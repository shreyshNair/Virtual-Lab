Key Generation in the DES Algorithm

Demonstrate generation of the 16 round-keys used by the Data Encryption Standard

Aim Theory Procedure Simulation Quiz References

Aim & Objectives

  • Understand how the DES key schedule derives sixteen 48-bit round keys from a single 64-bit key.
  • Demonstrate the role of Permuted Choice 1 (PC-1) and Permuted Choice 2 (PC-2) in the key schedule.
  • Observe the per-round left-circular-shift schedule applied to the two 28-bit key halves (C and D).
  • Generate and inspect all 16 round keys for a user-supplied or sample 64-bit key.

Theory

DES (Data Encryption Standard) uses a 64-bit key, of which only 56 bits are effective — the remaining 8 bits (every 8th bit) are parity bits and are discarded early in the key schedule. From these 56 bits, DES derives sixteen distinct 48-bit round keys K1…K16, one used in each of the algorithm's 16 encryption rounds.

  1. Permuted Choice 1 (PC-1): the 64-bit key is permuted and reduced to 56 bits, dropping the 8 parity bits.
  2. Split: the 56-bit result is split into two 28-bit halves, C0 and D0.
  3. Per-round left shift: before round i, both C and D are left-circular-shifted independently by 1 bit (rounds 1, 2, 9, 16) or 2 bits (all other rounds), producing Ci and Di.
  4. Permuted Choice 2 (PC-2): Ci and Di are concatenated (56 bits) and permuted/compressed down to the 48-bit round key Ki.
  5. Steps 3–4 repeat for all 16 rounds, each round carrying forward the previous round's C and D (not the original C0/D0).

Because each round key depends on the running Ci/Di state, the schedule is easy to compute forward but the 28-bit halves cycle back to C0/D0 after all shifts in a full 16-round pass (the shifts sum to 28).

Procedure

  1. Enter a 64-bit DES key as 16 hexadecimal digits, or click "Load sample key" / "Random key".
  2. Click Generate Round Keys.
  3. Observe the 64-bit key with its 8 parity bits highlighted.
  4. Observe the 56-bit key produced by PC-1, and its split into C0 and D0.
  5. Browse the table of all 16 round keys; click View on any row (or use Prev/Next) to see that round's shift and PC-2 derivation in detail.
  6. Attempt the quiz to test your understanding.
